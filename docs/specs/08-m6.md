# M6, first tier parity, rewritten as work

Milestone issue 8 in the library repository is M6. It has seven scope bullets and three exit criteria, and it is the largest milestone in the project by volume. This document is what those seven bullets look like once the surface has actually been counted, and it is what the issue's checklist becomes.

The change is not that M6 got bigger. It is that M6 was one issue with seven bullets and it should be eleven issues with a measurement behind each.

## What M6 covers, counted

| Workstream | Callables | Where the count comes from |
|---|---|---|
| A. Index and label based selection | 164 | `Index` 73 plus `MultiIndex` 86, minus overlap, plus `loc`, `iloc`, `at`, `iat`, `xs` |
| B. Strings and RE2 | 57 | the `.str` accessor, whole |
| C. Categoricals | 8 | `.cat`, plus `CategoricalDtype` and the `observed` and `ordered` paths |
| D. Nested data | 5 | `.list` 2 and `.struct` 3, which pandas covers thinly, plus `explode` and `json_normalize` |
| E. Windows | 53 | `Rolling` 22, `Expanding` 22, `ExponentialMovingWindow` 9 |
| F. Reshaping | 16 | `melt` through `wide_to_long`, top level and method forms |
| G. GroupBy completion | 32 | the 56 GroupBy callables minus the 24 that landed at M1 |
| H. Statistics and the rest of the frame | 61 | `rank`, `cut`, `describe`, `corr`, `interpolate`, `replace` and the rest of section 2 |
| I. The cheap surface | 40 | the arithmetic spellings, the aliases and the forgotten methods from document 02 |
| J. Error and warning types | 46 | `pandas.errors`, which is a prerequisite rather than a follow up |
| K. `api.types` and options | 45 plus 70 | the predicates every third party library calls, and the option keys |

That is roughly 530 callables, or 47 percent of the pandas surface, in one milestone. The M1 through M5 work covers about 230 more, M7 covers the temporal 250, and the remainder is M11 and the registered divergences. M6 is where the library becomes usable and it deserves eleven issues rather than seven bullets.

## The order, and why

**J first, before anything else in M6 lands.** The 46 exception types cost a day. Every operation written after they exist raises the right one at the point where it fails, and every operation written before them has to be revisited. Retrofitting error types across 500 callables is a task that does not happen, so this goes first and it is not negotiable on schedule grounds. It is also the smallest possible piece of work that improves L4 across the entire library at once.

**I second, for the same reason inverted.** The cheap surface is 40 names that are each ten minutes and that nobody will ever prioritise on their own. They are one issue, one pull request, and they move L0 and L1 further than any other single day in the milestone. Doing them early also means the conformance suite stops reporting `AttributeError` for things that are trivially present, which makes the rest of the milestone's numbers readable.

**A third, because everything leans on it.** `groupby(...).agg(...)` produces an indexed frame. `value_counts` produces an indexed series. `describe` produces an indexed frame. `unstack` produces indexed columns. Building those against a frame type with no index means building them twice. The parity checklist has the index as one bullet inside a list of seven and it is the largest single piece of work in the milestone after strings.

**B fourth, and whole.** The parity document already says the string namespace ships whole and it is right. RE2 is its own issue underneath it, because it is a regex engine and not a string method, and the eight `.str` methods that take a pattern are blocked on it while the other 49 are not. So B is two issues that run in parallel: the 49 that need no regex, and RE2 plus the eight.

**C, D, E, F, G, H in any order,** because they are independent of each other once A exists. E is the largest and the one with the most parameter interactions per callable, so it is the one most likely to be underestimated. F is the one that most benefits from A being finished first, since `unstack` and `pivot` are index operations wearing a reshape hat.

**K last in the milestone,** because a third party library calling `pd.api.types.is_numeric_dtype` on our frame only matters once the frame is worth handing to a third party library, which is M3 and after. It is in M6 rather than later because it is cheap and because leaving it out means every seaborn or scikit-learn call path is a surprise.

## Checklist, as it goes into the issue

Each of these is one issue in the library repository, and each has a conformance target rather than a description of doneness. The target is the L3 rate for that workstream's callables, measured by `pixi run conformance` in the compat repository, and the number is checked by the CI ratchet rather than by a person.

- J. Error and warning types, 46 types, L4 available on every operation that can fail
- I. The cheap surface, 40 callables, L1 100 percent and L3 100 percent
- A1. The `Index` type, 73 callables, L3 90 percent
- A2. `MultiIndex`, 86 callables, L3 70 percent
- A3. `loc`, `iloc`, `at`, `iat`, `xs`, and the frame methods that need an index, L3 100 percent
- B1. The 49 string methods that need no regex, L3 100 percent
- B2. RE2 and the eight pattern methods, L3 100 percent with the lookaround and backreference divergences registered
- C. Categoricals, 8 callables plus the dtype, L3 100 percent
- D. Nested data, `.list`, `.struct`, `explode`, `json_normalize`, L3 100 percent against pandas and the Polars superset registered as extensions rather than divergences
- E. Windows, 53 callables, L3 90 percent, with `win_type` weighted windows allowed to trail
- F. Reshaping, 16 callables, L3 100 percent
- G. GroupBy completion, 32 callables, L3 90 percent
- H. Statistics and the rest of the frame, 61 callables, L3 90 percent
- K. `api.types` predicates and the 70 option keys, L3 100 percent on the predicates and on the eight load bearing options

## Exit criteria, restated as numbers

The current issue says every `(M6)` checkbox is ticked with a runnable example, the differential test passes, and db-benchmark runs green at both sizes. Those are good criteria and two of the three are not measurable as written. Replaced with:

- The compat scoreboard reports L3 at or above the per workstream target for all eleven workstreams
- Parameter coverage is at or above 80 percent for every workstream, so that the L3 numbers mean something
- Zero failures. Unimplemented is a schedule, a failure is a bug, and a milestone does not close over a bug
- The pandas against pandas oracle run is perfect on the same commit
- Every divergence discovered during the milestone is registered with a reason before the milestone closes, and none of them is `kind = "pending"`
- db-benchmark green at 0.5 GB and 5 GB, published whatever it says, which is unchanged
- The operation cost matrix from document 09 exists for every workstream, with the losses published

## What M6 is still not

Time series stays in M7, all of it, including the time based rolling window and anything taking a frequency string. The boundary is that M6 owns everything that does not need a calendar.

`query` and `eval` stay in M11. They are a parser and a namespace resolution problem rather than a dataframe problem.

The Polars extensions to the nested accessors are in D as extensions, which means they are implemented and are not counted in the pandas denominator. An extension is not a divergence and the report keeps them apart, because a library that adds a method has not failed to match anything.
